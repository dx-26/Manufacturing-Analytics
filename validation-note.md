# Validation Note

I tested the manufacturing Skill against an edge case and a search failure. The edge case was an exploratory meeting with Badger Specialty Manufacturing. The only useful statement was that downtime was frustrating. The notes did not identify an asset, failure mode, baseline, data source, stakeholder, budget, owner, or date. An early draft still recommended a predictive-maintenance pilot, which sounded practical but was not supported by the meeting. I tightened the Skill so it cannot convert general interest into a commitment. The final output asks for the asset, failure mode, and discovery objective while labeling the owner and timeline `NEEDS HUMAN INPUT`. Its confidence is low. That behavior makes the gap obvious and keeps a salesperson from treating an invented next step as agreed.

For the search-failure test, I used `manufacturing AI news`. It returned a broad mix of robotics, generative AI, labor predictions, chip announcements, and vendor marketing. Even relevant-looking predictive-maintenance articles did not necessarily address false alarms, technician trust, CMMS workflow, or seal defects from the meetings. The output would have been a generic trend summary. I returned to Claude and refined the terms around the actual objections, including `site:rockwellautomation.com 2025 state smart manufacturing AI quality control 50%` and `site:siemens.com 2025 predictive maintenance technicians human expertise alerts`. The narrower search found Rockwell's finding that quality control was the leading AI use case in its 2025 survey, Siemens material explicitly keeping technicians central to diagnosis, and a Siemens customer example reporting avoided downtime. I labeled the Siemens result as vendor evidence from a different operation so it cannot be presented as guaranteed ROI.

I then ran all four meetings in one batch. Each generated memo contains at most two dated signals, and each signal shares at least one issue tag with that meeting. The test showed that keyword refinement improved both relevance and the quality of the recommended next step. It also showed why the human sign-off remains necessary: tag overlap cannot verify plant compatibility, data quality, cybersecurity, causation, or safety. Before external use, the account lead and a maintenance or manufacturing expert must verify every source, claim, owner, timeline, and proposed proof metric.
